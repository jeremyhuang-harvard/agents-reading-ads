# Literature map and research gap

## Positioning

Economics work frames LLMs as configurable simulated agents that may help explore theories and experiments, but this is a claim about a model system rather than a claim that completions are independent people.[2] Generative-agent architectures add memory, reflection, planning, and social interaction, showing how histories can alter later behavior without establishing behavioral or population fidelity.[3]

Persona-conditioned sampling has reproduced some subgroup patterns,[4] while direct audits show that plausible aggregate means can coexist with compressed variance, distorted subgroup relationships, and prompt sensitivity.[5] Survey answers can also change materially with answer labels and option ordering, so selective sensitivity—not blanket consistency—is a required validation target.[6]

Interview- and self-report-grounded agents offer a stronger route than invented demographic biographies, but their transfer to novel visual advertising and contextual interpretation remains an empirical question.[7] Marketing work has already used large AI-persona panels to replicate published media effects, so this project should not claim to introduce agents to advertising research.[8]

## Narrow gap

The defensible opening is a human-benchmarked design that:

1. holds an ad fixed;
2. systematically randomizes viewing context;
3. conditions agents on structured or empirically grounded histories and preferences;
4. measures inferred meaning before liking or purchase intent;
5. compares human and model **context effects and distributions**, not only average levels.

This connects salience-based consumer choice, where surrounding options alter which attributes receive attention,[9] to classic evidence that print-ad context changes salient product attributes and interpretation.[10]

## What not to claim

- Do not call prompted profiles representative consumers or digital twins.
- Do not treat repeated completions as independent respondents.
- Do not infer individual fidelity from aggregate agreement.
- Do not treat demographic persona wording as validated population sampling.
- Do not infer mechanism equivalence from a replicated treatment sign.
- Do not call text descriptions visual experiments when the model never receives pixels.
- Do not give working papers and preprints the status of peer-reviewed evidence.
- Do not claim advertising or media context is unstudied.

## Literature streams to expand

- salience, context-dependent choice, reference points, and constructed preferences;
- media and editorial context effects in advertising;
- LLMs as simulated economic agents;
- persona-prompt validity and survey-response artifacts;
- interview- and survey-grounded individual agents;
- multimodal model measurement and visual-attention proxies;
- human–LLM treatment-effect and distributional validation.
