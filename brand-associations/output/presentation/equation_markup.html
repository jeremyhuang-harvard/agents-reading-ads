Ads as Cues · the recall rule

Recall is a share: the target against everything else the cue brings to mind

rx(c, f) = S(c, x) · fxA Σx′ ∈ X S(c, x′) · fx′B  (1)
A · numeratorsimilarity of the cue to the target × how often the target was experienced. “The numerator captures associativity and frequency in recall.”
B · denominator = interferencethe same product summed over every memory, including the “fog” of neutral, non-branded experiences fn. “Experiences compete in recall… Interference captures distraction.”

Then recall over all cues a person sees is rg(e, f) = Σc rg(c, f) · ηc (2), and demand is dg = qg · rg − θ · pg (3): goods that are not recalled lose the pleasure qg from the purchase decision. Bordalo, Burro, Gennaioli, Nacamulli & Shleifer (2025), NBER WP 34387, eqs. (1)–(3).

The bridge to our data: an entity's association profile, the share of A's public memory that involves B, is an empirical stand-in for rB(c = A) in a representative population. Forward strength is rbrand(cue = celebrity); backward strength is rcelebrity(cue = brand). Both are this same fraction, read in opposite directions.

What changes relative to a product ad: a celebrity is a cue with a heavy, charged denominator. Pairing can raise term 1, but Novig still competes with everything she is, including politically loaded memories. That caps the forward link (it is why Tate is ≈0% of the way to Adidas) and decides what else comes along with the brand.
Backward · the cue is Novig

When a bettor sees “Novig”, what comes to mind? Crowding out

rbetting(Novig) = S(Novig, sports betting) · fbetting1 S·fbetting+S(Novig, Sweeney) · fSweeney2+Σcontroversy S·f3+S·fn
1 · what the brand is forThe category link a bettor needs (“where do I bet on football?” → Novig). Before the campaign, Novig's top searches were “novig betting”, “novig sportsbook”, Kalshi, Polymarket. observed
2 · the celebrity enters with her full frequencyfSweeney is enormous relative to anything Novig-specific, so even modest similarity makes her term dominate. Sweeney is 37% of Novig's related-search weight and 90% of its news; 26% of clicks leaving its Wikipedia page go to her, versus 8% to “Prediction market”. observed
3 · the controversy comes with her42% of Novig's clicks go to the three athletes who criticized the ad, 8% to “Shock advertising”. Anything retrieved through her (the jeans ad, her political associations) is reachable from the Novig cue as well. our proposal
Net effectThe numerator need not fall for the share to fall: the category link is diluted because the denominator has grown. That is crowding out in the model's own terms. our proposal
Caveat to say out loud: our numbers are shares, so we see dilution, not necessarily a fall in the category link's absolute strength. Novig's Wikipedia page did not exist before October 16, so there is no before-and-after click comparison.
What is the paper's, what is ours

Three additions to the recall rule

In the paper
X = consumption experiences of goods, plus neutral events n. Similarity S is symmetric. Interference = other goods and the fog.
Our proposal our proposal
A celebrity cue makes a structured block of non-consumption memories similar: her roles, partners, ads and controversies. These are part of X and enter the denominator.
Direction
Because f differs enormously between a star and a small brand, the same S gives rbrand(celebrity) ≪ rcelebrity(brand) for small brands. Across 53 ambassador pairs, the bigger memory absorbs the link (r = −0.55; on your pre-specified list, −0.48).
Valence our proposal
Recall in the paper retrieves the pleasure qg. If a brand cue retrieves a controversy, that retrieved content may lower simulated utility for some consumers and raise it for others (the AE stock rose after Trump's praise). Testable with search and sales by region or political lean.
